    About Agile Tracker

    What is Octopus Agile?

    Agile Octopus is a time-of-use electricity tariff from Octopus Energy where the unit rate changes every half hour, following the wholesale market. Next-day prices are usually published in the afternoon (around 4pm UK time). Prices can go negative when supply is high and demand is low, which means you are paid to use electricity.

    What is Agile Outgoing (the Export view)?

    Agile Outgoing is Octopus Energy's export tariff: it pays you per kWh you send back to the grid (solar, battery) at a half-hourly rate that follows the same wholesale market as Agile Octopus. Unlike Agile import it has no 4pm–7pm premium, so the best time to export is simply when wholesale prices are highest, often the evening peak or a cold, still morning. Export rates carry no VAT. In the Export view colours are reversed: green is a high (good) rate and red is a low or negative one, because on negative rates exporting costs you money. The 14-day outlook uses the same wholesale forecast with an export formula fitted to recent real Agile Outgoing rates, so it is a little less certain than the import view. You need an Agile Outgoing agreement and an export meter to be paid; this page only shows the rates.

    Why are prices higher between 4pm and 7pm?

    Agile adds a fixed premium (roughly 11–14p/kWh depending on region) between 16:00 and 19:00, when demand is highest. The price is also capped at 100p/kWh including VAT.

    How is the 14-day forecast calculated?

    Agile follows the wholesale day-ahead electricity price, so the forecast works in two steps. 1. Wholesale outlook. Three times a day a small machine-learning model predicts Great Britain's hourly day-ahead price for the next 14 days. It learns from about two years of history and looks at the UK wind, temperature and sunshine forecast (Open-Meteo, six sites across the country), National Energy System Operator's demand forecast, the gas price (National Gas's System Average Price), the recent price level, hour of day, weekday and bank holidays. 2. Your Agile price. Your region's formula, fitted to recent real Agile prices, turns each wholesale hour into half-hourly Agile rates: a regional multiple of the wholesale price, plus the 4pm–7pm peak charge, capped at 100p. When the real day-ahead prices are published, they replace the forecast.

    How to read it. Faded bars are forecast days and the thin line is the likely range (about 8 times in 10 the real daily average lands inside it). Weather forecasts lose skill after about a week, so days 8–14 show direction, not exact prices: on past data the daily average is off by roughly 12% the next day and 18–19% by day 14, and it calls the direction of big moves right about 8 times in 10. Each forecast day is a typical shape for that wholesale level, so half-hours are indicative only. Power station outages, gas price shocks and unusual weather can all move the real price. The model runs on a small server and its published file is public; your browser only downloads it and does the regional maths. It is an estimate, not advice.

    Where does the data come from?

    Import (Agile Octopus) and export (Agile Outgoing) prices come from Octopus Energy's public API. The explanation uses national generation mix and carbon intensity from the NESO Carbon Intensity API and regional weather from Open-Meteo. The 14-day outlook is a public JSON file (far.cx/uk-forecast.json) produced daily from NESO (day-ahead prices and demand forecast), Open-Meteo weather and National Gas gas prices. Your browser fetches everything directly; nothing about you is sent to us, and there is no account.
